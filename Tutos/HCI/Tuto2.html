HCI Tutorial 2: General Ergonomics Theories

Evaluating four screens with the ergonomic concepts of the course

Objectives

By the end of this tutorial, you should be able to:

  • Apply the 11 ergonomic concepts seen in the course (Miller, Hick, 2-second rule, 3-click rule, Fitts, affordance, proximity, similarity, colours, reading direction, text) to real interfaces.
  • Find where a concept is not respected on a screen, and recognise where it is respected.
  • Justify each observation with evidence taken from the screen.
  • Propose a realistic fix for every flaw found.
  • Argue your analysis in front of your classmates.

Toolbox · the concepts

ConceptRuleWhat to look for
Miller's magic number
Nombre magique de Miller		Working memory holds about 7 ± 2 items. Limit what must be remembered or chosen at once.Long lists, too many fields or items on one screen.
Hick's law
Loi de Hick		Decision time grows with the number and complexity of the options.Many choices at once, long menus, wordy messages.
2-second rule
Principe des 2 secondes		Never make the user wait more than 2 s without a response. Quick action: « done » indicator. Long action: « in progress » indicator.Clock icons, silent waits.
3-click rule
Principe des 3 clics		The information sought must be reachable in 3 clicks.Nested menus, detours, duplicated paths.
Fitts's law
Loi de Fitts		Time to reach a target depends on its size and its distance.Tiny or far buttons; a harmless button easier to hit than the main one.
Affordance
Potentialité		Properties of an object suggest what can be done with it. Clickable and non-clickable must be clearly distinct.Active elements that look disabled, links that look like buttons, unlabeled fields.
Law of proximity
Loi de proximité (Gestalt)		The brain groups elements that are physically close. Bring related things together, move different things apart.Unrelated items grouped; related items far apart.
Law of similarity
Loi de similarité (Gestalt)		The brain groups what looks alike (shape, colour, size, behaviour). Similar items share features; different items are marked.Same-looking items with different roles, or same data shown differently.
Colours
Couleurs		Strong contrast, at most 7 colours, usual meanings (green = success, red = alert/failure), same information = same colour, never colour alone.Weak contrast, errors that are not red, unrelated palettes.
Reading direction
Sens de lecture		First scan in a Z, then selective (F pattern). Main information visible without scrolling, in a logical order.Fields or actions in an illogical order, a flow that zigzags.
Text
Texte		Sans-serif fonts read best on screen; bold, italics, underline and CAPITALS slow reading; use few styles; write short, clear messages.Serif/monospaced fonts, ALL CAPS, technical or slangy messages.

Method

  1. Read the flow of the screen: follow the arrows from the menu to the last dialog; note the clocks (waiting times).
  2. Go through the 11 concepts one by one and look for a place on the screens where the concept is, or is not, respected.
  3. Justify: say what you see, where, and why it breaks (or respects) the concept.
  4. Fix: propose a realistic change.
  5. Compare with the solution (teachers only, protected by a code) and discuss the differences.

The four screens are real interfaces with deliberate flaws: each of them contains at least one error to find.
